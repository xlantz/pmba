Differentiation
Being different from competitors in ways customers value and rivals find hard to imitate; the escape from price competition.
Undifferentiated product
Must compete on price: low margins and weak bargaining power with the trade.
“Me-too” product
Head-to-head copy of a leader; often fails even with a big budget (e.g. Coke’s Surge, Vault, Mellow Yellow vs. Mountain Dew).
Levi’s LVC line
Historically focused $250+ line that “supports” and pulls up the whole brand image; authenticity no one else has.
Positioning to market leaders
Low-budget route: define your brand relative to one already in the consumer’s mind so the message cuts through clutter.
Target marketing
Focusing on particular segments or niches so the product can be the very best for them.
Benefits of targeting
Less competition, higher margins, higher loyalty and repeat purchase, focused design, focused communications, lower marketing costs.
Retargeting
Showing ads again to a person who already showed interest (e.g. visited the site), as with a Facebook sponsored post.
Attraction (decoy) effect
A clearly dominated option draws choice toward the option that dominates it, often the high-end one.
Consideration set
The few brands a buyer seriously evaluates; a hierarchical shortcut that limits information processing.
Selective attention / retention / distortion
Noticing, remembering, and interpreting information in line with existing attitudes.
Mental accounting
Treating money in separate “accounts” (e.g. “I haven’t spent on entertainment lately”), which can justify a purchase.
Satisficing
Choosing a good-enough option with minimal effort; typical of low-involvement purchases (vs. optimizing).
Low involvement
Minimal effort, in-store decision, 1–2 brands, brand critical, ads emphasize frequency, intensive distribution.
High involvement
Considerable effort, 4–6 products considered, optimizing, ads emphasize reach (print, search), selective distribution.
Clinites
Markstrat’s B2C cosmetics market: frequently purchased; five segments, four channels, six attributes.
Base cost
Average transfer price (COGS) for the first 2.5 million units.
85% experience curve
Unit cost falls to 85% of its prior level each time cumulative production doubles.
Markstrat budget rule
Next period budget = 40% of net contribution (EBT), with a floor and a ceiling.
Production adjustment
Production is automatically adjusted ±20% against your plan; you pay for units only when sold.
Distribution margin (Markstrat)
Channel’s cut as a % of actual retail price: 30% mass, 35% specialized and portals, 40% department stores.
Unit gross contribution
Selling price to the channel minus transfer cost (e.g. $9.45 − $4.75 = $4.70 in mass merchandisers).
%ACV
Volume-weighted percent of outlets that carry your brand (distribution panel).
Semantic scales study
Gives the perceptual map: brand perceptions, each segment’s ideal points, attribute importance.
Case method routine
Situation → market (5 Cs) → customer → offering → options with numbers → recommendation.
