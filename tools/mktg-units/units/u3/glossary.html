Behavioral segmentation
Segmenting on what people do: needs/benefits, purchase stage, and user status or value.
Psychographic segmentation
Attitudes, interests, opinions (AIO), values and lifestyle.
Firmographics
The B2B equivalent of demographics (industry, company size, and so on).
Purchase stage variables
Aware/unaware, tried/never tried, loyal/non-loyal, current/former, upsell/cross-sell targets.
RFM
Recency, frequency, monetary value: a user-status/value segmentation profile.
B2B segmentation
More geography, deeper needs knowledge, support level, the customer’s customer (derived demand), the buying center.
Complete segment profile
Benefits (drives ad creative) + demographics (drives media) + psychographics (tone). E.g. “Everyday Loyals” wine buyers.
Factor analysis (in segmentation)
Groups the survey questions into a few underlying factors (e.g. lifestyle factors).
Cluster analysis
Groups the people by their factor scores into segments.
Harley-Davidson segments
Tour Gliders, Dream Riders, Hard Core, Hog Heaven, Zen Riders, Live to Ride (from 72 agree/disagree statements).
Dream Riders
Largest Harley segment (30.8%); bike mostly parked, short trips, stock bike; fewest miles (3,675/yr).
Live to Ride
Riding is a total lifestyle; most miles (9,662/yr) and riding days (214).
Perceptual objectives
The position you tell Markstrat your advertising should establish on the perceptual map.
Semantic-scale ideal value
A segment’s ideal on a 1–7 scale; regression charts convert it to real units (5.6 ≈ $20.50 price for High Earners).
Online query (Markstrat R&D)
Returns the required budget and base cost for a proposed product before you create the project.
R&D project too expensive
Smaller budget over 2 periods, weaker attributes, or accept a higher base cost and re-query.
Voice of the customer (VOC)
Using customers’ own words to find needs, how well they are met, and language that resonates.
Topic modeling
Finds the main themes in a text corpus; most reflect needs or benefits sought.
Aspect-based sentiment analysis
Scores each topic in a review positive or negative separately from the overall review.
Importance by frequency
Topics mentioned more often are assumed more important (image quality 80%, battery 78%); the easy default.
Importance by relationship
How strongly topic sentiment moves the overall star rating; reveals true drivers of (dis)satisfaction.
Parsimonious segmentation
Fewest segments that are homogeneous within and heterogeneous between.
Customer language bank
Short representative customer phrases to reuse in messaging.
Opportunity map
Ranks opportunities by prevalence, pain, rating impact, unmet need, fit, differentiation, feasibility, confidence.
VOC deliverables
(1) VOC analysis, (2) laddering exercise and creative brief, (3) ad execution and pitch.
L’Oréal case frameworks
Benefit segmentation, “advanced + everyday” positioning, brand architecture and class-to-mass risk, global adaptation.
