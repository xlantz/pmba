Dream Riders are the biggest segment (30.8%) but ride the least (3,675 miles and 109 days a year), so growth there means giving them reasons to ride (events, short-trip routes) rather than a new bike. Tour Gliders own the most Harleys (4.74) and favor touring bikes, so they are the core for comfort-oriented touring models and accessories.

The assignment

Take the role of a major camera manufacturer or a start-up and develop a new marketing campaign for a point-and-shoot digital camera. Data: full-text Amazon reviews from the 2023 UCSD archive. That is 9M+ electronics reviews, of which 184,000 are for point-and-shoot cameras and 18,187 were posted from 2020 to 2023. The Excel file has everything scraped from the site minus some sparse columns; 2023 is recent enough to treat as current.

What VOC analysis is

It uses the customer’s own words to understand their needs, how well those needs are met, and how they might respond to new features, positioning or messaging. Insights can emerge that the company never thought to ask about, and the customer’s language can be reused so benefits are described in words that resonate.

1. Needs/benefits analysis

Start from a text corpus (here, reviews). Topic modeling finds the main themes, which mostly reflect needs or benefits sought. Aspect-based sentiment analysis then scores each topic positive or negative separately, regardless of the review’s overall tone.

Example from the slides

“The camera takes beautiful pictures, but the battery life is terrible.” The customer wants image quality and battery life; the first need is met, the second is not. If many customers say the same about many products, there is a market-wide unmet need, and an opportunity.

Positive sentiment = need met; negative = need unmet. Unmet needs are openings for new features, positioning or messaging. Needs are broader than specs: performance, ease of use, fit with a usage occasion, the buying process, fit with complementary products, perceived value.

How important is each need? Two measures

By the end of the needs analysis you should know the different needs, which are met and unmet, how important each is as a driver of satisfaction, the key pain points, and where expectations and experience don’t match.

Types of needsSigns of an unmet need
Functional (what it must do); emotional (how they want to feel); social (how they want to be seen, sharing); contextual (situations of use); practical constraints (budget, skill, portability, age, technical comfort, connectivity, durability, time pressure)Repeated complaints; workarounds; trade-offs accepted reluctantly; things expected but not received; comparisons to smartphones or older cameras; gaps between specs and real value; the same frustration described without a solution

2–6. The remaining analyses

  • Segmentation: group customers by the benefits they emphasize (benefit segments, as in the L’Oréal case). Aim for a parsimonious number: homogeneous within, heterogeneous between. Too few and different customers share a segment; too many and two segments should really be one. Count customers per segment. Possible segments include travelers, family memory keepers, beginners, smartphone upgraders, older or less technical users, gift recipients, photographers wanting a small second camera, zoom-focused users, vloggers, budget buyers, and point-and-shoot replacers. Demographic guesses from AI (from words like “grandkids”) may not be very accurate.
  • Journey: trigger, research and comparison, who is involved, channel (mostly Amazon), unboxing and setup, everyday use, travel or special events, sharing photos, support and returns, long-term ownership.
  • Competitive analysis: per brand: review volume, average stars, core strengths, common complaints, differentiating themes, where each is strong or vulnerable, and where a premium manufacturer could credibly differentiate.
  • Customer language bank: short representative phrases about motivation, desired outcomes, pain points, benefits, smartphone comparisons, travel, family, beginner use, quality and reliability, value and price.
  • Opportunity map: rank opportunities by prevalence, pain intensity, impact on ratings, degree of unmet need, strategic fit, differentiation potential, feasibility, and your confidence in the evidence.

Limitations and tips (from the slides)

  • It’s hard to know how GPT reaches conclusions, and hard to keep it to the data; told the reviews are about cameras, it will name image quality as a key need almost regardless. Don’t let it be lazy, and keep it doing the work (a Pro subscription balked at all 184K reviews).
  • Review titles carry a lot of information; check that they are analyzed.
  • Talk to it with follow-ups (e.g. “Among reviewers who mention portability, are they comparing to a phone or a DSLR?”). Let AI do the volume work. Ask it to describe a VOC analysis first, check what’s missing and add guard rails (stick to the review text), then have it write the analysis prompt.
