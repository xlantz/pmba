Frameworks that apply (all from this unit’s chapter or earlier units)

  • The pricing methods (Ch 11, “Selecting a Pricing Method”): markup and target-return pricing start from cost; economic-value-to-customer pricing starts from what the offering is worth to the buyer; competitive and auction pricing start from the market. Which anchor fits the case, and what price does each give?
  • Customer value (Ch 11 and Unit 6): what is the offering worth to the customer compared with the next-best alternative, including total cost of ownership?
  • Consumer psychology of price (Ch 11): reference prices, image pricing (price as a quality signal) and price thresholds. Could a low price signal low quality to these buyers?
  • Final price and product-mix pricing (Ch 11): price discrimination, bundling and unbundling, and how a price fits the rest of the line.
  • Break-even (Unit 2): at each candidate price, how many units are needed to cover fixed costs?
  • Business buying (Unit 6): who in the buying center cares about price, and who cares about performance or risk?

Discussion questions

  • Compute a price under at least two methods (cost-based and value-based). Why do they differ?
  • What is the customer’s next-best alternative, and what is the offering worth relative to it?
  • How would the main competitor respond to each price?
  • Would bundling, unbundling or price discrimination (Ch 11) change your recommendation?
  • Which price would you choose, and what evidence would change your mind?
