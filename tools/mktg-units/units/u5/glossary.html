Pass-through
The share of a trade promotion that retailers pass on to shoppers as lower prices; often low.
Forward buying
Retailers stock up at the deal price and sell later at regular price, keeping the discount.
Promotion lift decomposition
Category incidence 22%, brand choice 60%, purchase quantity 18%.
Promotion vs. advertising duration
Promotion effects last about 2 weeks; advertising effects about 39 weeks.
Permanent promotion effects
Much likelier in emerging than mature markets; any long-term effect is mainly from category incidence.
Problems with sales promotions
Little long-term impact, cheapened image, deal-sensitivity and switching, low pass-through, discounts to people who’d buy anyway.
Good use of price promotion
Generating trial for a superior product; a strong brand is the key to managing the trade.
Conjoint analysis
Measures the value of each attribute (incl. price) and how consumers trade attributes off, from ratings or rankings of whole products.
Why not ask for the ideal?
Consumers want infeasible bundles (275 yards, 54 holes, $1.25); the ideal says nothing about must-haves vs. can-go.
Orthogonal design
A balanced subset of profiles (9 credit cards instead of 81) so each attribute’s effect can be estimated separately.
Part-worth utility
The value of one level of one attribute; part-worths sum to value a whole product.
Attribute importance
Range of an attribute’s part-worths ÷ sum of all ranges. Credit cards: interest 59%, fee 24%, brand 10%, limit 7%.
