Integrated marketing communications (IMC)
Coordinated use of communication tools that work in concert and reinforce one another to reach strategic goals.
Horizontal integration
Coordinating packaging, pricing, promotions and distribution with the communication campaign.
Vertical integration
Aligning communication objectives with the company’s higher-level marketing strategy.
Internal integration
Sharing information from product development, research, sales and service with the communication team.
External integration
Coordinating with agencies, event organizers and co-sponsors.
Coverage
Share of the audience each option reaches, and the overlap among options.
Contribution
An option’s ability to produce the desired response on its own, without other exposures.
Commonality
The extent different options reinforce the same associations (shared meaning).
Complementarity
Different options emphasize different associations that together build the position.
Conformability
How well an option works for consumers who have and have not seen the other communications.
Advertising
Presentation and promotion by an identified sponsor using paid media.
TV’s three strengths
Demonstrates attributes and benefits; portrays imagery and personality; captive audiences at live events.
Print ad elements
Picture, headline, copy, in that order of importance; under 50% notice even an outstanding ad.
Radio’s main advantage
Flexibility: targeted stations, cheap production and placement, short scheduling lead times.
Brand safety
Risk of ads or AI content appearing beside, or becoming, offensive content (e.g. KFC Germany).
Native advertising
Paid content matching the look and feel of the publication: content recommendations, in-feed ads, promoted search listings.
Contextual placement
Buying online ads on sites related to your own offerings.
Microsite
Individual page or cluster of pages supplementing a primary site (e.g. insurer’s used-car advice).
Search engine optimization (SEO)
Improving the chance your link ranks high among non-paid results; cheaper than SEM.
Search engine marketing (SEM)
Paying search engines to appear for keywords; usually pay per click.
Place (out-of-home) advertising
Billboards, public spaces, product placement, point of purchase.
“15-second sell”
Billboards: exposure is fleeting, so the message must be grasped almost instantly.
Product placement
Paid or supplied cameo appearances in film and TV (Bond: ~$100M of value in No Time to Die).
Point of purchase (P-O-P)
Communication in store, where many brand decisions are made.
Social media platforms
Online communities and forums, blogs, social networks, customer reviews.
Customer reviews
Often the second most trusted source of brand information, after friends and family; negatives weigh more.
Viral marketing
Content consumers pass along online; follows the rules of entertainment more than selling.
Influencer marketing
A popular online figure promotes an offering to their social circle; mix of publicity and paid endorsement.
Influencer effectiveness drivers
Originality, follower size, sponsor salience; weaker for new-product launches.
Influencer fraud
Bought followers and engagement; over 60% of Instagram mega-influencers inflated figures.
Mobile’s four characteristics
Single user; always with them and on (geolocation); linked to payment; highly interactive.
Location-specific promotion
Offers sent to opted-in customers near a store or outlet.
Event marketing
Exhibits, displays and presentations showcasing the offering, in person or livestreamed.
Experiential marketing
Connecting the offering with unique experiences so customers feel how it fits their lives.
Sponsorship selection criteria
Fits objectives, enough awareness, right image, audience matches target, unique, few sponsors.
Earned media
Unsolicited professional commentary, personal blogs and social discussion generated by paid and owned efforts.
Online vs. offline WOM
Interesting brands get talked about online; salient, visible brands dominate face-to-face talk.
Publicity
Promotion through editorial space (no media cost); credible but not controllable.
Public relations (PR)
Programs that promote or protect a company’s image and reputation among stakeholders.
PR department functions
Press coverage, corporate communications, lobbying.
PR media formats
Publications, events, news, speeches, public service activities, identity media.
BBVA case frameworks
Marginal analysis across channels, the six IMC criteria, digital measurability, paid/owned/earned, CLTV.
