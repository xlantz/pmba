Marketing communications
How firms inform, persuade and remind consumers about their products and brands; the voice of the brand.
Communication macromodel
Nine elements: sender, receiver, message, media, encoding, decoding, response, feedback, noise.
Noise
Random and competing messages that interfere with the intended communication.
Learn-feel-do
Response sequence for high involvement with meaningful differentiation (car, house).
Do-feel-learn
High involvement, little differentiation (airline tickets, PCs).
Learn-do-feel
Low involvement, little differentiation (salt, batteries).
Response steps
Awareness, knowledge, liking, preference, conviction, purchase.
Communication objective
A specific task and achievement level for a specific audience in a specific period.
Recognition vs. recall
Recognition (easier) matters in store; recall matters when the brand isn’t in view.
Primary vs. selective demand
Awareness of the need vs. awareness of the specific brand.
Comparative advertising
Explicitly compares brands; works best when it engages cognition and emotion together.
Reinforcement communication
Convinces current buyers they made the right choice.
Quantitative & temporal benchmarks
How much the objective must achieve, and by when.
Objective-and-task budgeting
Define objectives, the tasks to reach them and their costs; the sum is the budget. Recommended.
Competitive-parity budgeting
Matching competitors’ spending; flawed because competitors aren’t wiser and situations differ.
Target audience vs. target market
Can differ: cereal ads to kids (adults buy), milk ads to adults (kids drink).
Core selling proposition
A good ad focuses on one or two.
Creative brief (Ch 12)
Key message, target audience, objectives (to do, know, believe), key benefits, support, media.
Eight communication modes
Advertising, online/social, mobile, direct marketing, events/experiences, WOM, publicity/PR, personal selling.
Paid / owned / earned / shared media
Paid for; owned channels; outsiders talk voluntarily; company content posted on social channels.
Mobile (Ch 12)
Timely, pervasive and customized.
Reach (R)
Number of different people or households exposed at least once in the period.
Frequency (F)
Average number of exposures per person or household in the period.
Impact (I)
Qualitative value of an exposure in a given medium.
Gross rating points (GRPs)
E = R × F; 80% reach × 3 = 240 GRPs.
Weighted exposures (WE)
R × F × I.
Cost per thousand (CPM)
Media cost per thousand persons reached; adjust for audience quality, attention, editorial quality, placement.
Continuity / concentration
Even exposure all period / all spending in one period (one season or holiday).
Flighting / pulsing
On-off-on bursts / a low continuous base with periodic heavier waves.
Areas of dominant influence
The markets bought in “spot buys” (vs. national and local buys).
Informational appeal
Attributes or benefits: problem-solution, demonstration, comparison, testimonial.
Transformational appeal
Non-product benefit or image (user imagery, experience); often emotional.
Two-sided message
Mentions shortcomings; better with educated or initially opposed audiences.
Fear appeals
Work best when not too strong, the source is credible, and relief is believable.
Source credibility
Expertise, trustworthiness, likability.
Q score
Entertainment-industry rating of a celebrity’s familiarity and likability.
Principle of congruity
When a liked source praises a disliked brand, attitudes toward both move toward each other.
ADPLAN
Attention, distinction, positioning, linkage, amplification, net equity (Rucker, Kellogg).
Linkage
Whether people remember the brand and benefit, not just the ad (Ameriquest failed it).
Amplification
Whether viewers’ thoughts about the ad are positive or negative.
Net equity
Whether the ad fits the brand’s heritage and established associations.
Supply-side measurement
Media coverage (seconds on screen, column inches); ignores message content.
Demand-side measurement
Effect on the audience: recognition, recall, feelings, attitude change, behavior.
Share of voice
Company’s advertising for a product ÷ all advertising of similar competing products.
Historical approach
Correlates past sales with past advertising to estimate sales impact (vs. experiments).
Red Bull seeding program
Micro-targeting “in” venues and opinion leaders before broad distribution and mass media.
Week 8 deliverable
VOC laddering exercise and creative brief; check it with one or two selling propositions and ADPLAN.
