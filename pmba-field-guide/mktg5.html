Conducting marketing research and forecasting demand

Marketing research turns raw data into insight. Follow five steps (define the problem, plan, collect, analyze, decide), pick primary or secondary data and the right approach and instrument, sample sensibly, then use the same logic to size and forecast demand and to track results with metrics and dashboards.

The definition, in plain words

Marketing research is the function that links the consumer, customer, and public to the marketer through information. That information is used to identify and define marketing opportunities and problems; generate, refine, and evaluate marketing actions; monitor marketing performance; and improve understanding of marketing as a process. The researcher specifies the information needed, designs the collection method, manages the data collection, analyzes the results, and communicates the findings and their implications.

Data is not insight

Marketing insights are diagnostic information about how and why we see certain effects in the marketplace and what that means for marketers. Raw numbers or an expert’s opinion are not enough.

CaseWhat happenedLesson
WalmartA large study of U.S. shoppers found its edge was the functional benefit “offers low prices” plus the emotional benefit “makes me feel like a smart shopper.” It replaced “Always Low Prices. Always.” with “Save Money, Live Better.”Insight became a campaign.
TropicanaRedesigned its orange-juice package (dropped the orange-with-straw image) without adequately testing consumer reactions. Sales fell 20%; the old design returned after a few months.No insight, big loss.
Star WarsA 1970s research executive predicted the film would fail (post-Watergate realism, “war” in the title). He never studied the script. It grossed over $4.3 billion at the box office.He delivered information and opinion, not insight.

Companies also misuse research when they do not understand what it can do, give the researcher a problem definition that is not specific enough, or hold unrealistic expectations.

Who does marketing research

Most companies budget marketing research at 1 to 2 percent of company sales and spend a large share of it on outside firms. Many large companies also have in-house departments (P&G’s Consumer & Market Knowledge department, whose global strategy line is “Consumer is Boss”).

Also: watch competitors (Staples founder Tom Stemberg and IKEA founder Ingvar Kamprad made unannounced or incognito store visits) and tap employees (“two-pizza” teams of four to six people who observe customers).

AI and marketing research

Three benefits: (1) operational efficiency (writing questionnaires, translating, coding open-ended answers, charts and reports, adaptive surveys, even AI moderators for online focus groups); (2) real-time insights (L’Oréal’s TrendSpotter scans millions of comments, images, and videos; social listening); (3) predictive analytics (Kellogg’s scans trending recipes; Mondelez built a model on its panel of “supertasters”). Two cautions: data quality (GenAI can “hallucinate,” and errors in the input data show up in the output) and credibility (managers may distrust algorithms they do not understand and ignore them).

Good marketing research is characterized by the scientific method, creativity, multiple research methods, accurate model building, cost-benefit analysis, healthy skepticism, and an ethical focus (chapter summary).

Good marketers follow a formal process with five steps (Figure 5.1). The chapter threads one running case through it: American Airlines deciding whether to offer ultra-high-speed Wi-Fi to first-class passengers on long-haul flights.

Step 1: Define the problem (not too broad, not too narrow)

VersionExample from the chapterProblem
Too broad“Find out everything you can about first-class air travelers’ needs.”Collects lots of unnecessary information
Too narrow“Find out whether enough passengers on a B777 between Chicago and Tokyo would pay $25 for ultra-high-speed Wi-Fi so we break even in one year.”Rules out useful questions (why $25? why break even?)
Just right“Will offering ultra-high-speed Wi-Fi create enough incremental preference and profit to justify its cost against other service enhancements American might make?”Focuses on the key issue yet is actionable

The business question then splits into four sub-questions (offer it at all? first class only or also business/economy? what price(s)? which planes and trip lengths?) and five research objectives: (1) which types of first-class passengers respond most; (2) how many would use it at different prices; (3) how many might choose American because of it; (4) how much long-term goodwill it adds to the image; (5) how important it is relative to other services such as a power plug or enhanced entertainment.

Three types of research

TypeGoalAA example
ExploratoryIdentify the problem and suggest possible solutionsListen to how travelers talk about carriers
DescriptiveQuantify demandHow many first-class passengers would buy Wi-Fi at $25?
CausalTest a cause-and-effect relationshipDoes price ($25 vs $15) change usage?

Step 2: Develop the plan, and check the cost

The plan is the most efficient way to gather the needed information and an estimate of what it will cost. You must decide on: data sources, research approaches, research instruments, sampling plan, and contact methods (the next three sections). The cost of the research has to be in line with its estimated benefits.

Chapter example: how much is research worth?

American estimated that launching Wi-Fi would yield a long-term profit of $50,000. If managers believe the research will lead to a better pricing and promotional plan and a higher long-term profit, they should be willing to spend up to $40,000 on it. If the study would cost more than $40,000, it is not worth doing.

General illustration: a $25,000 quote is under the $40,000 cap, so go.
A $55,000 quote is over the cap, so shrink the study or skip it.

Qualitative vs. quantitative, at a glance

Chapter facts behind the line: focus groups and open-end questions are exploratory; qualitative techniques are indirect and unstructured, with small samples and results that may not generalize (different researchers can draw different conclusions); survey and behavioral data are best at counting; experiments are for cause and effect. Marketers do not have to choose: for example, a panel member can complete an in-home product test with a video diary and an online survey.

Exam angleGiven a scenario, name the approach (for instance, “three weeks of price changes on one route” is an experiment; “mining scanner data” is behavioral). Know why behavioral data can beat stated answers and why focus-group findings should not be generalized.

Three main instruments collect primary data: questionnaires, qualitative measures, and technological (measurement) devices.

Questionnaires

A questionnaire is a set of questions presented to respondents. It is by far the most common primary-data instrument because it is flexible. Form, wording, and sequence all influence responses, so test and de-bug it. Closed-end questions list every possible answer (easy to interpret and tabulate). Open-end questions let people answer in their own words, which suits exploratory research (how people think, rather than how many think a certain way).

Measurement devices and neuromarketing

Skin sensors, brain-wave scanners, full-body scanners, eye-tracking, facial-recognition software (estimates age and gender), and biometric wrist sensors that read electrodermal activity (skin conductance). Neuromarketing is brain research on the effects of marketing stimuli, using EEG and fMRI. Findings: Super Bowl ads with the highest brain activity differed from those with the highest stated preference; brain waves predicted music purchases better than stated preferences. Cautions: expensive, not universally accepted, should not be the sole basis for decisions, and the devices can be obtrusive (electrode caps, artificial exposure conditions).

Exam angleName a question type from its example (Likert vs semantic differential vs intention-to-buy), pick the right qualitative technique for a described task, and arrange a laddering chain: attribute, functional benefit, emotional benefit, core value.

Data collection is generally the most expensive and error-prone phase: some respondents are away, offline, or inaccessible and must be re-contacted or replaced; others refuse or give biased or dishonest answers. A sampling and data-collection plan controls cost while keeping quality high.

The sampling plan: three decisions

After choosing the sampling unit, build a sampling frame so everyone in the target population has an equal or known chance of being sampled. Probability sampling lets researchers calculate confidence limits for sampling error and makes the sample more representative. Intercept interviews risk nonprobability (non-random) samples.

Online research examples

Del Monte used its 400-member, handpicked “I Love My Dog” community; the result (Snausages Breakfast Bites, bacon-and-egg taste plus vitamins and minerals) launched in half the usual time. Kraft online communities helped develop its 100-calorie snacks. SurveyMonkey (founded 1999, over 60 million registered users), Survey Gizmo, Qualtrics, and Google Consumer Surveys are online survey providers. Danone uses social listening across hundreds of millions of messages (trend: “food as medicine”; Williams pears for Blédina; lactose-free demand in Spain for Activia).

Share of search (SOS)

Using Google Trends data, a brand’s share of search is its portion of category searches compared with competitors. It is easier and cheaper to get than market-share data and is an early predictor of market share. One study found changes in share of search came before changes in market share by about 3 months (energy brands), 6 months (mobile phone handsets), and 9–12 months (autos). Rolling averages add stability. Caveat: search turns into sales only if the full marketing program works (price, availability, product match).

General illustration: reading share of search

Suppose a phone brand’s share of category searches climbs from 20% to 25% this quarter. By the chapter’s timing finding for handsets, you would watch for market share to follow in roughly six months, if price, distribution, and the product itself are all working.

Data mining

Marketing statisticians extract useful information about individuals, trends, and segments from the mass of available data using: cluster analysis (group objects so those in one cluster are more alike than those in others), predictive modeling (forecasting outcomes of uncertain events), and cognitive modeling (simulating human decision making and problem solving with a computer model).

Use of customer dataWhat it means
Identify prospectsAds with a response feature (link, reply card, toll-free number) build a database; sort for the best prospects and convert them
Decide who gets an offerDefine the ideal target for the offer, search the database for lookalikes, and track response rates. Up-selling = a more expensive version; cross-selling = complementary products. Example sequence: thank-you email at 1 week, new offer at 5 weeks, discount at 10 weeks if no response
Deepen loyaltyRemember preferences; send appropriate gifts, coupons, reading material
Reactivate purchasesAutomatic marketing: birthday and anniversary cards, holiday reminders, off-season promotions
Avoid serious customer mistakesA bank penalized a major depositor for a late mortgage payment, let two staff pitch the same customer different prices, and gave a premium customer standard service abroad

Step 4: Analyze the information

Tabulate the data and develop summary measures: averages and measures of dispersion for the main variables, then advanced statistical techniques and decision models. Test different hypotheses and theories, and use sensitivity analysis to test the assumptions and the strength of the conclusions.

Step 5: The AA Wi-Fi numbers, worked through

Other findings: passengers would use it mostly to stay connected and send or receive large documents and emails (some would download video) and would charge it to their employers; Wi-Fi would strengthen American’s image as innovative and progressive and win some new passengers and goodwill.

The decision belongs to managers. Low confidence in the findings may mean no launch; a predisposition to launch finds support in the findings; they may also ask for more research. Draw a line between market data and market insights: data alone are not very useful unless they improve managers’ understanding and the cost-effectiveness of their actions (“Information is not knowledge,” said Einstein).

Research finds opportunities; the company must then measure and forecast the size, growth, and profit potential of each. The sales forecast is used by finance (raising cash), manufacturing (capacity and output), purchasing (supplies), and human resources (hiring). A forecast that is off the mark leaves the company with excess or inadequate inventory.

Key concepts in demand measurement

ConceptChapter definition
Market demandTotal volume that could be bought by a defined customer group, geographic area, time period, marketing environment, and marketing program
Company demandEstimated share of market demand at alternative levels of company marketing effort; depends on how your products, services, prices, and communications are perceived relative to competitors
Market forecastMarket demand projected for a future time period
Company sales forecastExpected level of company sales based on a chosen marketing plan and an assumed environment
Total market potentialMaximum sales achievable in an industry in a period, at a given level of industry marketing effort and given environment; the limit approached as industry marketing spending approaches infinity
Company sales potentialExpected company sales for a period based on market trends and company marketing effort; less than market potential in most cases because each competitor has a hard core of loyal buyers
Sales quotaSales goal for a product line, division, or rep; a managerial device, often set slightly higher than estimated sales to stretch the sales force
Sales budgetConservative estimate of expected sales for purchasing, production, and cash-flow decisions; generally set slightly lower than the sales forecast

Formula: total market potential

The chapter’s common estimate: potential number of buyers × average quantity each buyer purchases × price.

General illustration: 200,000 buyers × 3 units each × $50 price
= 600,000 units × $50 = $30,000,000 market potential

Chapter point: compare your growth with the industry’s

If your sales rise 5% a year while industry sales rise 10%, you are losing relative standing in the industry (share is shrinking). Industry totals often come from trade associations (who usually do not list individual firms) or from audit firms such as NielsenIQ. B2B marketers know less about share because distributors will not report competitors’ sales.

General illustration: you sell 100, industry sells 1,000 (10.0% share)
Next year: you sell 105, industry sells 1,100 (105 / 1,100 = 9.5% share)

Forecasting: the order of work and the three information bases

Forecasting is the art of anticipating what buyers are likely to do under a given set of conditions. Firms usually prepare a macroeconomic forecast first (inflation, unemployment, interest rates, consumer spending, business investment, government expenditures, net exports), ending in a GDP forecast; then an industry forecast; then the company sales forecast, assuming the company wins a certain market share. Surveys of buying intentions feed consumer confidence (the Conference Board) and consumer sentiment (University of Michigan Survey Research Center) measures. Firms can build forecasts or buy them (marketing research firms; long-range specialists such as IHS Global Insight, Forrester Research, Gartner Group; futurist firms such as the Institute for the Future, Hudson Institute, and the Futures Group produce speculative scenarios).

Why it is hard

Inputs (marketing expenses and investments) are easy to quantify in the short run, but outputs such as brand awareness, brand image, customer loyalty, and new-product prospects may take months or years to show up, and internal or external changes can happen at the same time and blur the effect.

Marketing metrics are the set of measures that help marketers quantify, compare, and interpret marketing performance. Choose metrics based on the issues you face. Mindbody tracks landing-page conversions, click-through rates for online ads, and Google search rankings, and watches weekly (1) website analytics, (2) social media presence, and (3) permission marketing statistics from automated emails.

HorizonTypical measures
Short-term resultsProfit-and-loss concerns: sales turnover, number of customers, market share
Long-term impactCustomer attitudes and behaviors, brand power, customer loyalty and retention; internal metrics like innovation (3M tracks the share of sales from recent innovations)

Marketing dashboards

A marketing dashboard is “a concise set of interconnected performance drivers to be viewed in common throughout the organization” and works like a car or plane instrument panel with real-time indicators. It should include two market-based scorecards: one for performance and one for early-warning signals. Dashboards are only as good as their data. Marketing controllers may review budget items and expenses, using business intelligence software.

Pick metrics on purpose: the wrong metrics can make managers overlook opportunities and threats and steer the company wrong, so the choice belongs in strategic planning, not as an afterthought based on whatever is easy to observe.

Heineken’s “Good Times Index”

Built with academics from several disciplines around Freddy Heineken’s line “I don’t sell beer. I sell gezelligheid (good times).” Five measures (A Feeling of Open-mindedness, Experiencing Elevated Moments, Quality Socializing, Balance & Moderation, Sense of Purpose), each with three drivers. After validation with consumers (87% said having a good time is more important than ever), the resulting 15 drivers joined the ongoing brand tracking survey.

5 measures × 3 drivers each = 15 drivers
