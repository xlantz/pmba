Accounting from zero

If you have never taken accounting, read this page once, top to bottom (about 15 minutes). It gives you the handful of ideas every unit builds on, then a map of the course so you know which page to open next. Every unit page has the same route: Start here → Learn it → Cheat sheet → Practice, with an optional Go deeper section for the harder homework-level material.

Idea 1

Accounting is the scoreboard for a business

Accounting records what a business owns, owes and earns, and turns it into a few standard reports so outsiders can judge it.

Investors decide whether to buy shares. Banks decide whether to lend. Managers decide what to fix. None of them can look inside the company, so they read its financial statements. Everything in this course is either (a) how a number on those statements gets created, or (b) how to read the statements once they exist.

Think of a sports season

Each play is a transaction. The scoreboard is the statements. Accounting is the rulebook for how plays are counted, so two teams' scoreboards mean the same thing.

Idea 2

Four statements, one story

That is why a profit can exist without cash (a sale on credit) and cash can exist without profit (a loan). Units 1 and 2 teach you to read the statements; Unit 3 shows how they are built.

Idea 3

Accrual accounting: count it when it happens

The one rule that surprises beginners

A caterer finishes a $10,000 event on June 28 and the client pays July 15. On a cash basis, the revenue shows up in July. On the accrual basis used by companies in this course, revenue is recorded when it is earned (June), and expenses are recorded in the period they helped earn that revenue (the matching principle). That's why the statements contain accounts like Accounts Receivable, Prepaid Insurance and Wages Payable: they are the bridge between "it happened" and "cash moved".

Idea 4

Debits and credits are just left and right

Every transaction touches at least two accounts and keeps the equation balanced. Debit means the left side of an account, credit the right. Neither means good or bad.

Account typeIncrease with
Assets, expensesDebit
Liabilities, equity, revenueCredit

Example: you sell $500 of services for cash. Debit Cash 500 (asset up), Credit Revenue 500 (revenue up). Total debits always equal total credits.

Your map

The course, unit by unit

Do them in order the first time. Each page opens with a plain-language Start here, then the test-core sections, then an optional Go deeper.

Unit 1: Reporting Foundations

The equation, the three activities, and the ratios (margin, turnover, ROA).

Unit 2: Statements & SEC Filings

Each statement, how they link, cash vs. accrual, audits.

Unit 3: The Accounting Cycle

Journal entries, adjusting entries, roll-forwards, closing.

Unit 5: Revenue & Receivables

When revenue counts, deferred revenue, bad debts, operating expenses.

Unit 6: Inventory & Long-Lived Assets

FIFO/LIFO, turnover, depreciation, intangibles.

Unit 7: Liabilities

Contingencies, warranties, notes, mortgages, bonds, debt ratios.

Unit 8: Equity, Dividends & EPS

Issuing stock, dividends, splits, treasury stock, stock pay, EPS.

Unit 9: Intercorporate Investments

Fair value, equity method, consolidation, NCI, goodwill.

Study route for the test: unit page (Start here + ★ sections) → cheat sheet → the professor's problems on Exam Prep Practice → mock test. When you want a stretch, the homework practice is the deep end: it is a lot harder than the exams.
