Module 1 & 2 · Q1Conceptual Question

Which of the following best describes the fundamental accounting equation?

How to work it
  1. The fundamental accounting equation is Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders' Equity — this is the definition every other topic builds on.
  1. B. Assets = Liabilities + Stockholders’ Equity

Module 1 & 2 · Q2Conceptual Question

A company reports total assets of $800 million and total liabilities of $500 million. Which statement is correct?

How to work it
  1. Stockholders' equity is the residual interest: Assets − Liabilities = $800M − $500M = $300M, which owners hold a claim to after creditors are satisfied.
  1. C. Owners have a $300 million residual interest in the company’s assets.

Module 1 & 2 · Q3Conceptual Question

Which transaction increases both assets and stockholders’ equity but does not affect net income?

How to work it
  1. Issuing common stock for cash increases both cash (an asset) and contributed capital (equity) by the same amount, with no revenue or expense recorded — net income is unaffected.
  1. B. Issuing common stock for cash

Module 1 & 2 · Q4Conceptual Question

Which of the following represents financing provided by nonowners?

How to work it
  1. Nonowner financing comes from liabilities. Of the options, only accounts payable is a liability — common stock and retained earnings are equity, and net income is an income statement figure, not financing.
  1. C. Accounts payable

Module 1 & 2 · Q5Conceptual Question

Return on assets (ROA) is most useful for evaluating:

How to work it
  1. ROA (net income ÷ average assets) measures how efficiently a company converts its asset base into profit.
  1. B. How effectively a company generates profit from its asset base

Module 1 & 2 · Q6Conceptual Question

Two companies have identical ROAs. Company A has a higher profit margin, while Company B has a higher asset turnover. Which conclusion is most appropriate?

How to work it
  1. Since ROA = Margin × Turnover, two companies can reach the identical ROA through different combinations — one earning more per dollar of sales, the other turning over assets faster.
  1. C. The companies are achieving similar overall returns through different operating strategies.

Module 1 & 2 · Q7Conceptual Question

A company’s profit margin increases while its asset turnover remains unchanged. All else equal, its ROA will most likely:

How to work it
  1. ROA = Margin × Turnover. If margin rises and turnover holds steady, the product (ROA) must rise as well.
  1. A. Increase

Module 1 & 2 · Q8Conceptual Question

A company’s asset turnover increases. This generally indicates that the company is:

How to work it
  1. Asset turnover = Revenue ÷ Average Assets, so a higher turnover means more revenue is being generated per dollar of assets employed.
  1. A. Generating more revenue relative to the assets it employs

Module 1 & 2 · Q9Conceptual Question

Company A has a higher profit margin than Company B but a lower asset turnover. Which statement is most accurate?

How to work it
  1. Knowing Company A has a higher margin and lower turnover than Company B isn't enough — since ROA = Margin × Turnover, the specific numbers determine which product is larger. You need more information.
  1. C. Additional information is needed to determine which company has the higher ROA.

Module 1 & 2 · Q10Conceptual Question

Why is average total assets generally used in the denominator of ROA rather than ending total assets?

How to work it
  1. Net income is earned by using assets throughout the entire period, so an average balance (not a single ending snapshot) better represents the resources actually employed to generate it.
  1. C. Net income represents activity throughout the period, so an average asset balance better represents the resources employed during that period.

Module 1 & 2 · Q11Conceptual Question

Why is average stockholders’ equity generally preferable to ending stockholders’ equity when calculating ROE?

How to work it
  1. The same logic applies to ROE: net income accrues to shareholders across the whole year, so average equity is the more representative denominator than a single point-in-time ending balance.
  1. B. Net income is earned throughout the year, while ending equity reflects only one point in time.

Module 1 & 2 · Q12Conceptual Question

A company provides $75,000 of services to customers on account. Which statement is correct at the time the services are provided?

How to work it
  1. Performing a service on account means the obligation is satisfied even though cash hasn't arrived yet — revenue is recognized, and accounts receivable (not cash) increases to reflect the amount owed.
  1. B. Revenue and accounts receivable both increase.

Module 1 & 2 · Q13Conceptual Question

A company later collects cash from a customer whose receivable was recognized in a prior period. The collection will:

How to work it
  1. Collecting cash on an existing receivable just converts one asset into another (AR down, cash up) — the revenue was already recognized back when the sale occurred, so it isn't recorded again.
  1. B. Increase cash and decrease accounts receivable

Module 1 & 2 · Q14Conceptual Question

Which transaction decreases retained earnings but does not reduce net income?

How to work it
  1. A cash dividend is a distribution of already-earned profit to shareholders, not an expense — it reduces retained earnings and cash directly without touching net income.
  1. C. Paying a cash dividend

Module 1 & 2 · Q15Conceptual Question

Which financial statement reports a company’s assets, liabilities, and stockholders’ equity at a specific date?

How to work it
  1. The balance sheet is a snapshot: assets, liabilities, and equity as of one specific date, unlike the income statement or cash flow statement, which summarize activity over a period.
  1. C. Balance sheet

Module 1 & 2 · Q16Conceptual Question

Which of the following would normally be classified as a current asset?

How to work it
  1. A current asset is one expected to convert to cash or be used within a year. Of the choices, only inventory fits — the rest are liabilities.
  1. C. Inventory

Module 1 & 2 · Q17Conceptual Question

Which of the following would normally be classified as a long-term asset?

How to work it
  1. Property, plant, and equipment (PP&E) is used over many years, making it a long-term asset — the other options are all liabilities.
  1. B. Property, plant, and equipment

Module 1 & 2 · Q18Conceptual Question

An auditor issues an unqualified (unmodified) opinion on a company’s financial statements. This means the auditor believes:

How to work it
  1. An unqualified (unmodified) opinion is the auditor's conclusion that the financial statements are presented fairly, in all material respects, under the applicable accounting framework — it says nothing about fraud, completeness of testing, or the company's financial health.
  1. C. The financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the company’s financial position and results in accordance with the applicable accounting framework.

Module 1 & 2 · Q19Conceptual Question

A company receives an unqualified opinion on its financial statements but an adverse opinion on internal control over financial reporting. Which conclusion is most appropriate?

How to work it
  1. These are two separate, independent opinions. A company's financial statements can still be fairly presented (unqualified FS opinion) even if the auditor separately finds a material weakness in internal controls (adverse ICFR opinion).
  1. B. The financial statements may be fairly presented even though material weaknesses exist in internal control.

Module 1 & 2 · Q20Conceptual Question

Which statement best describes retained earnings?

How to work it
  1. Retained earnings is a cumulative, running total: all net income earned since the company began, minus all dividends paid out over that same time — not a cash reserve or a market value.
  1. B. Cumulative earnings retained in the business, adjusted for items such as dividends

Module 1 & 2 · Q38Computational Question

Module 1 & 2 · Q40Computational Question

Module 3 · Q1Conceptual Question

What is the primary purpose of an adjusting entry at the end of an accounting period?

How to work it
  1. Adjusting entries exist specifically to ensure revenues and expenses are recognized in the period they're actually earned or incurred, not necessarily when cash moves.
  1. B. To ensure revenues and expenses are recognized in the appropriate accounting period

Module 3 · Q2Conceptual Question

A company has employees who have earned wages by December 31 but will not be paid until January. The December 31 adjustment should:

How to work it
  1. Wages earned but not yet paid require an accrual: increase (debit) wages expense and increase (credit) a wages payable liability — cash isn't touched until January.
  1. B. Increase an expense and increase a liability

Module 3 · Q3Conceptual Question

A company pays for a one-year insurance policy in advance. At the date of payment, the unexpired portion of the policy is best classified as:

How to work it
  1. The unexpired portion of a prepaid insurance policy still represents future economic benefit the company hasn't consumed yet, which is exactly the definition of an asset.
  1. C. An asset

Module 3 · Q4Conceptual Question

When a company records depreciation expense, which of the following occurs?

How to work it
  1. Recording depreciation increases (debits) depreciation expense and increases (credits) accumulated depreciation, a contra-asset — it never touches cash or the equipment's historical cost directly.
  1. C. An expense increases and accumulated depreciation increases.

Module 3 · Q5Conceptual Question

A company receives cash from a customer before providing the promised service. At the time the cash is received, the company generally records:

How to work it
  1. Cash received before a service is performed means the company owes the customer a service, not money — that obligation is recorded as a liability (unearned/deferred revenue).
  1. C. A liability

Module 3 · Q6Conceptual Question

Why is cash received in advance from a customer generally considered a liability?

How to work it
  1. A cash advance obligates the company to provide goods or services in the future — that future obligation is exactly what makes it a liability, not revenue.
  1. B. The company has an obligation to provide goods or services in the future.

Module 3 · Q7Conceptual Question

Which situation represents an accrued expense?

How to work it
  1. An accrued expense is one that's been incurred but not yet paid. Employees who have worked but haven't been paid yet is the classic example — the other options either involve cash already paid, or cash received in advance.
  1. C. Employees have worked but have not yet been paid.

Module 3 · Q8Conceptual Question

Which situation represents accrued revenue?

How to work it
  1. Accrued revenue is revenue that's been earned but not yet received in cash — interest that has accumulated but hasn't been collected fits exactly.
  1. A. A company earns interest that has not yet been received in cash.

Module 3 · Q9Conceptual Question

If a company fails to record an adjusting entry for wages that employees have earned but have not yet been paid, which of the following will occur?

How to work it
  1. Omitting the wage accrual means both sides of that entry are missing: wages expense stays too low (understated) and wages payable also stays too low (understated).
  1. B. Expenses will be understated and liabilities understated.

Module 3 · Q10Conceptual Question

If a company fails to recognize the portion of prepaid insurance that has expired during the period:

How to work it
  1. If expired prepaid insurance isn't recognized, the prepaid insurance asset stays too high (overstated) and insurance expense stays too low, which means net income is too high (overstated).
  1. A. Assets and net income will both be overstated.

Module 3 · Q11Conceptual Question

If a company fails to recognize revenue that has now been earned from a previously recorded unearned revenue balance:

How to work it
  1. If earned revenue isn't recognized out of unearned revenue, the liability stays too high (overstated, since it should have decreased) and revenue stays too low (understated).
  1. B. Liabilities will be overstated and revenue understated.

Module 3 · Q12Conceptual Question

A company reports positive net income but negative operating cash flow. Which statement is most appropriate?

How to work it
  1. Under accrual accounting, revenue can be recognized before cash is collected and expenses before cash is paid — so positive net income and negative operating cash flow can coexist due to this timing gap; it isn't an error.
  1. C. The company may have recognized revenues that have not yet been collected in cash or incurred expenses that have not yet been paid.

Module 3 · Q13Conceptual Question

A company performs services on account. What is the immediate effect?

How to work it
  1. Performing a service on account increases accounts receivable and revenue together — no cash is involved yet.
  1. B. Accounts receivable and revenue increase.

Module 3 · Q14Conceptual Question

Which of the following best explains why accrual-basis net income can differ from net cash flow?

How to work it
  1. Accrual accounting recognizes economic activity based on when it's earned or incurred, not strictly when cash changes hands — that timing difference is exactly why net income and cash flow diverge.
  1. A. Accrual accounting recognizes economic activity based on when it is earned or incurred rather than solely when cash changes hands.

Module 3 · Q15Conceptual Question

What happens to temporary accounts during the closing process?

How to work it
  1. Closing entries reset every temporary account (revenues, expenses, dividends) to zero, so each new period starts with a clean slate.
  1. A. Their balances are transferred so that they begin the next accounting period at zero.

Module 3 · Q16Conceptual Question

Which account is a permanent account and therefore is not closed at year-end?

How to work it
  1. Retained earnings is a permanent account — it's never closed, and it carries its balance forward indefinitely, unlike revenue and expense accounts.
  1. D. Retained earnings

Module 3 · Q17Conceptual Question

After all closing entries have been completed, which account should normally have a zero balance?

How to work it
  1. After closing, Service Revenue (a temporary account) is reset to zero, since its balance was closed into retained earnings.
  1. C. Service revenue

Module 3 · Q18Conceptual Question

Which statement best describes the relationship between depreciation expense and accumulated depreciation?

How to work it
  1. Depreciation expense is the current period's cost, reported on the income statement; accumulated depreciation is the running, cumulative contra-asset balance reported on the balance sheet.
  1. B. Depreciation expense is a period expense, while accumulated depreciation is a contra-asset account on the balance sheet.

Module 3 · Q19Conceptual Question

When inventory is purchased on account:

  1. Purchasing inventory on account increases both inventory (an asset) and accounts payable (a liability) — cash and revenue aren't involved at the purchase point.
  1. A. Inventory and accounts payable both increase.
