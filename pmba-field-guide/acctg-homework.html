For each transaction (a–g), select + for an increase, − for a decrease, or n/a if the line item isn't affected.
a. Issued common stock for cash and property & equipment   b. Paid cash for rent of office furnishings/facilities   c. Performed services, received cash immediately   d. Performed services, billed client (due in 60 days)   e. Paid cash salary to an employee   f. Received partial cash payment on the receivable from (d)   g. Paid cash dividends

How to work it
  1. Balance Sheet: Cash moves with every cash transaction (a in part, b, c, e, f cash-in, g). Noncash assets move when property or receivables change (a's property portion, d's new receivable, f's receivable collected). Contributed capital only moves with stock issuance (a). Retained earnings moves with anything hitting net income (b, c, d, e) or dividends (g) — not with pure asset swaps like (f).
  2. Cash flow statement: Only cash transactions show up. Financing CF: the cash portion of the stock issuance (a) and the dividend (g). Operating CF: cash rent (b), cash service revenue (c), cash salary (e), and the cash collected on the receivable (f). Investing CF is untouched here — the property in (a) was acquired via stock, not cash, so it's a noncash transaction.
  3. Income statement: Revenue is recognized when services are performed (c and d), regardless of when cash arrives — that's why (f), which is just collecting cash on an existing receivable, has no income statement effect. Expenses hit when incurred (b, e), reducing net income.

  6. f. Collected cash on the receivable: increases Cash and Operating cash flow; decreases Noncash assets. No income statement effect.
  7. g. Paid cash dividends: decreases Cash, Retained earnings, and Financing cash flow. Nothing increases.
