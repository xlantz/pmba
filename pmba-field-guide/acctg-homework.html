FY3: %

How to work it
  1. FY4 profitability is 28.5% — positive net income means the company was profitable.
  2. Profitability fell from 31.0% (FY3) to 28.5% (FY4), so FY3 was the more profitable year.

PepsiCo, Inc. and The Coca-Cola Company are the two largest beverage companies in the world. Which company has the better ROA?
How to work it
  1. PepsiCo: Average Total Assets = (107,399 + 99,467) ÷ 2 = 103,433. ROA = 8,240 ÷ 103,433 ≈ 8.0%. Margin = 8,240 ÷ 93,925 ≈ 8.8%. Turnover = 93,925 ÷ 103,433 ≈ 0.91x. Check: 8.8% × 0.91 ≈ 8.0% ✓
  2. The Coca-Cola Company: Average Total Assets = (104,816 + 100,549) ÷ 2 = 102,682.5. ROA = 13,107 ÷ 102,682.5 ≈ 12.8%. Margin = 13,107 ÷ 47,941 ≈ 27.3%. Turnover = 47,941 ÷ 102,682.5 ≈ 0.47x. Check: 27.3% × 0.47 ≈ 12.8% ✓
  3. Coca-Cola's ROA (≈12.8%) beats PepsiCo's (≈8.0%) — and notice how it gets there: Coca-Cola's margin (27.3%) is more than triple PepsiCo's (8.8%), while its turnover (0.47x) is roughly half of PepsiCo's (0.91x). This reflects a real business-model difference: Coca-Cola is largely a concentrate/syrup licensor, pushing most of the capital-intensive bottling and distribution work (and its thinner margins) onto independent and partially-owned bottlers. PepsiCo owns much more of its own manufacturing and distribution directly (including Frito-Lay, Quaker, and Gatorade), which adds revenue and asset turnover but compresses margin.
  4. This is the same margin-vs-turnover tradeoff from the DuPont decomposition on the main Unit 1 page — two very different paths to a ROA advantage.

The following information is available for AMD and Intel for a recent fiscal year (practice figures).
• AMD's assets increased by $650 million and its liabilities increased by $210 million.
• Intel's assets increased by $3,200 million and its liabilities decreased by $540 million.

a. Complete the table.
Assets, beg.Assets, endLiabilities, beg.Liabilities, endStockholders' Equity, end
AMD $2,900 $2,400
Intel $95,000 $41,000
b. Calculate average assets for each company.
AMD:
Intel:
c. Which company has the larger proportion of its assets financed by the company's owners at year-end?
How to work it
  1. Ending assets = beginning assets + change. AMD: 2,900 + 650 = 3,550. Intel: 95,000 + 3,200 = 98,200.
  2. Ending liabilities = beginning liabilities + change. AMD: 2,400 + 210 = 2,610. Intel: 41,000 − 540 = 40,460.
  3. Stockholders' equity = Assets − Liabilities. AMD: 3,550 − 2,610 = 940. Intel: 98,200 − 40,460 = 57,740.
  4. Average assets = (beginning + ending) ÷ 2. AMD: (2,900+3,550)/2 = 3,225. Intel: (95,000+98,200)/2 = 96,600.
  5. Owner-financed proportion = Equity ÷ Assets. AMD: 940/3,550 ≈ 26.5%. Intel: 57,740/98,200 ≈ 58.8%. Intel is higher.

U2-Q2Balance Sheet Equation and Financing Sources

Unit 2

TechMart's recent financial statements, dated January 28, report total assets of $9,150 million and total liabilities of $8,400 million (practice figures).

a. Why might TechMart have chosen January 28 as a year-end date?
b. What is the amount of TechMart's equity at January 28?
$
c. Does TechMart receive more financing from its owners or nonowners?
d. What percentage of financing is provided by TechMart's nonowners? (one decimal place)
%
How to work it
  1. Retailers often pick a fiscal year-end shortly after the holidays so the full holiday sales season is captured before the books close for the year.
  2. Equity = Total Assets − Total Liabilities = 9,150 − 8,400 = 750.
  3. Liabilities (8,400) are far larger than equity (750), so nonowners provide most of the financing.
  4. Nonowner % = Liabilities ÷ Assets = 8,400 / 9,150 ≈ 91.8%.

Crocker Corporation began its most recent fiscal year with stockholders' equity of $250,000, consisting of contributed capital of $190,000 and retained earnings of $60,000. During the year, it issued additional stock for cash proceeds of $50,000, reported net income of $85,000, and paid $40,000 in cash dividends. (Practice figures.)

How to work it
  1. Each row updates the running total: Contributed Capital only changes from stock issuance; Retained Earnings only changes from net income and dividends.
  2. Issuance of stock: +50,000 to Contributed Capital, no effect on Retained Earnings.

U2-Q5Reading and Interpreting Audit Opinions

Unit 2

Amazon.com, Inc.'s financial statements include the following audit report (real report, unchanged).

The Board of Directors and Shareholders Amazon.com, Inc. Opinion on the Financial Statements We have audited the accompanying consolidated balance sheets of Amazon.com, Inc. (the Company) as of December 31, 2025 and 2024, the related consolidated statements of operations, comprehensive income, stockholders' equity, and cash flows for each of the three years in the period ended December 31, 2025, and the related notes (collectively referred to as the "consolidated financial statements"). In our opinion, the consolidated financial statements present fairly, in all material respects, the financial position of the Company at December 31, 2025 and 2024, and the results of its operations and its cash flows for each of the three years in the period ended December 31, 2025, in conformity with U.S. generally accepted accounting principles. We also have audited, in accordance with the standards of the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (United States) (PCAOB), the Company's internal control over financial reporting as of December 31, 2025, based on criteria established in Internal Control-Integrated Framework issued by the Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (2013 framework) and our report dated February 5, 2026 expressed an unqualified opinion thereon.
a. To whom is the audit report addressed?
b. True or False for each statement:
1. Auditors express an opinion on whether the statements present fairly and are free from material misstatement
2. Auditors express an opinion that there was no fraudulent activity at the company
3. Auditors' opinion is that there are no misstatements in the related notes
4. Auditors separately audited internal controls over financial reporting
5. Auditors examined most of the company's financial transactions individually to ensure they were accurate
c. What sort of opinion did the auditor provide on the effectiveness of internal control over financial reporting?
d. Which best describes the scope of the two opinions in the report?
How to work it
  1. The greeting line of the report names exactly who it's addressed to — read the first line: "The Board of Directors and Shareholders, Amazon.com, Inc."
  2. Audits assess fair presentation and material misstatement, not fraud detection or line-by-line transaction verification — that's why statements 2, 3, and 5 are false, and 1 and 4 are true (a separate internal-controls opinion is explicitly mentioned in the second paragraph).
  3. The report says the internal controls opinion was "unqualified" — stated directly in the last sentence ("expressed an unqualified opinion thereon"). Worth noting: this is a genuinely clean bill of health on controls, unlike some companies (e.g., newer public companies) that sometimes receive an adverse ICFR opinion while still getting a clean opinion on the financial statements themselves.
  4. The report contains two distinct opinions: one on the fairness of the financial statements (first paragraph), one on internal control effectiveness (second paragraph) — they are not the same scope, even though both came back clean here.

Unit 2

Baiman Corporation commences operations at the beginning of January. It provides services on credit and bills customers $150,000 for January sales, unpaid at month-end. Employees earn January wages of $42,000, not paid until February 1. Complete the month-end statements. (Practice figures — no negative signs except for net loss/negative retained earnings.)

Income Statement
Sales
Wages expense
Net income (loss)
Balance Sheet
Cash
Accounts receivable
Total assets
Wages payable
Retained earnings
Total liabilities and equity
How to work it
  1. Sales are billed but unpaid — revenue is still recognized under accrual accounting (150,000), but no cash changes hands, so it becomes Accounts Receivable rather than Cash.
  2. Wages are earned but unpaid — the expense is recognized (42,000) but creates a liability, Wages Payable, rather than reducing Cash.
  3. Net income = Sales − Wages expense = 150,000 − 42,000 = 108,000. With no beginning balance, Retained Earnings = Net income = 108,000.
  4. No cash transactions occurred at all this month, so Cash = 0. Total assets = Cash + AR = 0 + 150,000 = 150,000, which equals Wages payable + Retained earnings = 42,000 + 108,000 = 150,000.

U2-Q7Applying Financial Statement Linkages to Understand Transactions

Unit 2

For each transaction (a–g), select + for an increase, − for a decrease, or n/a if the line item isn't affected.
a. Issued common stock for cash and property & equipment   b. Paid cash for rent of office furnishings/facilities   c. Performed services, received cash immediately   d. Performed services, billed client (due in 60 days)   e. Paid cash salary to an employee   f. Received partial cash payment on the receivable from (d)   g. Paid cash dividends

How to work it
  1. Balance Sheet: Cash moves with every cash transaction (a in part, b, c, e, f cash-in, g). Noncash assets move when property or receivables change (a's property portion, d's new receivable, f's receivable collected). Contributed capital only moves with stock issuance (a). Retained earnings moves with anything hitting net income (b, c, d, e) or dividends (g) — not with pure asset swaps like (f).
  2. Cash flow statement: Only cash transactions show up. Financing CF: the cash portion of the stock issuance (a) and the dividend (g). Operating CF: cash rent (b), cash service revenue (c), cash salary (e), and the cash collected on the receivable (f). Investing CF is untouched here — the property in (a) was acquired via stock, not cash, so it's a noncash transaction.
  3. Income statement: Revenue is recognized when services are performed (c and d), regardless of when cash arrives — that's why (f), which is just collecting cash on an existing receivable, has no income statement effect. Expenses hit when incurred (b, e), reducing net income.

How to work it
  1. Retained earnings rolls forward like a running balance: beginning balance, plus increases, minus decreases, equals ending balance.
