Personal selling and direct marketing

Personal selling is the most effective tool late in the buying process: a six-step selling process, a deliberately designed sales force (objectives, strategy, structure, size, pay), and active management (recruit, train, motivate, evaluate). Direct marketing goes further by reaching customers without intermediaries through mail, catalogs, phone, and TV, with responses you can measure.

Chapter 14, section by section

What personal selling is

Personal selling is direct interaction with one or more prospective buyers to make presentations, answer questions, and procure orders. It is the most effective promotion tool at the later stages of the buying process, when it shapes buyer preference, conviction, and action. Mass and digital communications can create awareness, but a person is often needed to be relevant and close the sale.

Examples: Mary Kay and Avon sell person-to-person (a rep demonstrates products at a host's home and takes orders). Multilevel (network) marketing recruits independent distributors, whose pay includes a percentage of sales made by the people they recruit plus earnings on their own direct sales.

Three qualities of personal selling

QualityMeaning
CustomizedThe message can be tailored to appeal to any individual.
Relationship orientedRelationships range from matter-of-fact to deep personal friendship.
Response orientedThe buyer gets personal choices and is encouraged to respond directly.

Example: Mr. Weston

California realtor Garrett Weston built a personal brand ("Mr. Weston") through tailored suits, an active web and social presence with local market insight, and personalized property visits. The chapter credits this strong personal brand with helping him sell over one billion dollars of residential real estate. The point: in personal selling, the salesperson is the brand.

AI and marketing: sales

Sales has moved from passive order-takers to active order-getters, and AI is accelerating that shift (Microsoft's Dynamics 365 Copilot and Salesforce's Einstein GPT launched one day apart in March 2023). The chapter lists four applications:

ApplicationWhat AI does
Observing and coachingConversation intelligence listens (with permission) to calls and reviews emails, chats, and CRM notes to find which phrases top reps use and which value propositions resonate, then coaches the whole team.
Relationship buildingGenAI drafts personalized outreach; alerts and reminders (e.g., a contact changed companies) give a reason to reach out, signal a possible new account, and warn the old account may be at risk.
Operations automationAccount sizing, quotas, forecasting, reports, call-note summaries, and pipeline scoring (prioritizing deals by likelihood to sign) are automated.
Orchestration with marketingAI supports account-based marketing (ABM) (e.g., flagging accounts without senior contacts so marketing can target them) and lead nurturing with personalized emails.

Concerns: job security (Meta's 2023 layoffs eliminated some ad account-management roles because AI took over tasks), impersonal customer experience, and unreliable data distorting forecasts, contracts, or rep pay. One industry report predicted 60% of B2B sellers' work would use GenAI by 2028, up from under 5% in 2023.

Exam angleKnow the three qualities of personal selling (customized, relationship oriented, response oriented) and match an AI example (conversation intelligence, pipeline scoring, ABM alerts) to its application category.

Most sales training agrees on six steps. Think of it as a funnel from finding a possible buyer to keeping the customer.

Step by step

StepWhat happens
1. Prospecting and qualifyingIdentify and qualify leads so reps spend their expensive time selling. IBM uses BANT: does the customer have budget, authority to buy, a compelling need, and a timeline that fits? Many firms now use analytics and AI on hiring patterns, job boards, and tweets. Balance lead quantity against quality: too many overwhelms reps, too few or poor leads demoralizes them.
2. Pre-approachLearn the prospect company (needs, who takes part in the decision, how purchasing is structured) and its buyers (characteristics, buying styles). Understand the purchase process in terms of who, when, where, how, and why; set call objectives (qualify the prospect, gather information, or make an immediate sale); choose a contact approach (visit, phone, email, letter); plan an overall account strategy.
3. Presentation and demonstrationTell the story with FABV: Features, Advantages, Benefits, Value (details below). Reps often over-emphasize features (product orientation) instead of benefits and value (customer orientation).
4. PersuasionSelling is interactive: buyers raise questions and objections, from psychological resistance or logical resistance. Price is the most frequently negotiated issue.
5. ClosingWithout closing there is no sale. Watch for closing signs (physical actions, comments, questions), then close and build a long-term relationship.
6. ServicingFollow up after closing: confirm delivery and terms, schedule a post-delivery call, detect problems, and plan to maintain and grow the account.

FABV with a concrete example (laptop)

LetterMeaning
FeaturePhysical characteristic: "16 GB of memory and a fast processor."
AdvantageAttribute relative to competitors: "Twice the memory of the rival model."
BenefitFunctional, monetary, or psychological gain: "Your analysts finish models faster and stop waiting."
ValueWorth, the sum of benefits and costs: "Saves more in staff time than the premium price costs."

Laptop wording is an illustrative example; the chapter's own example is only that a computer's features include processing speed and memory capacity.

Handling objections and closing

TopicDetails
Psychological resistanceResistance to change, habits, inertia, reluctance to give up benefits, negative associations, decision fatigue, anticipated regret.
Logical resistanceObjections to price, delivery schedule, or product or company characteristics.
Ways to respondStay positive; ask the buyer to clarify the objection; ask questions so the buyer answers his own objection; deny the objection's validity; or turn it into a reason for buying.
Closing techniquesAsk for the order; recap points of agreement; offer to help write up the order; ask "A or B?"; get minor choices (color, size); state what the buyer loses by not ordering now; offer an inducement (extra service, quantity, token gift); or move to a more senior decision maker.

From transaction to relationship: SPIN

Most selling and negotiation principles are transaction oriented, but companies often want long-term supplier-customer relationships. For key customers, reps should contact them outside order time, make useful suggestions, and monitor accounts. SPIN is a popular approach built on four question types: Situation, Problem, Implication, and Need-payoff questions.

Exam anglePut the six steps in order; decode BANT, FABV, and SPIN from a scenario; and classify an objection as psychological or logical ("it's too expensive" is logical; "we've always used the old vendor" is psychological).

Salespeople are the company's personal link to customers. Designing the sales force means five decisions: objectives, strategy, structure, size, and compensation. This section covers the first three.

Objectives: the six tasks

TaskMeaning
Information gatheringMarket research and intelligence work.
TargetingDeciding how to allocate time among prospects and customers.
CommunicatingConveying information about the company's products and services.
SellingApproaching, presenting, answering questions, overcoming objections, closing.
ServicingConsulting, technical assistance, arranging financing, expediting delivery.
AllocatingDeciding which customers get scarce products during shortages.

Reps today must diagnose problems and propose solutions that improve the customer's profitability, even uncovering unrecognized needs. Marketing and sales often conflict (sales says marketing doesn't produce enough leads; marketing says sales doesn't convert them). Ford's CEO Jim Farley runs both functions and sees top Ford salespeople as part problem solver, part concierge.

Strategy: direct vs. contractual sales force

TypeDescription
Direct (company) sales forceFull- or part-time paid employees working exclusively for the company. Inside salespeople work from the office; field salespeople travel to customers.
Contractual sales forceManufacturers' reps, sales agents, and brokers who earn commission on sales.

Worked example: furniture maker

A North Carolina furniture maker wants to reach West Coast retailers. Option A: hire 10 reps in a San Francisco office (salary plus commission). Option B: use a San Francisco sales agency with 30 reps on commission. The decision has three steps:

  1. Estimate the sales volume each option would generate (a company force is focused and better trained; the agency has more reps, contacts, and independence).
  2. Estimate the cost of selling different volumes through each. The agency starts cheaper but its costs rise faster because of larger commissions.
  3. Compare: at one break-even sales level (labeled SR in Figure 14.3) costs are equal. Below SR the agency is cheaper; above SR the company sales force is cheaper. That is why agents suit smaller firms, or large firms in small-volume territories.

Control problem with agencies: they may focus on the biggest-buying customers rather than on your goods, and may not master the technical details or handle promotion well.

Structure

One product line sold to one industry across many locations suggests a geographic territory structure. Many products sold to customers with diverse needs suggests organizing by product, service, or customer need. Complex firms combine four types: a strategic market sales force for major accounts, a geographic force, a distributor force that calls on and coaches distributors, and an inside force that takes orders online and by phone. A leveraged sales force has reps focus on complex, customized products for large accounts while inside sales and online ordering handle low-end selling (lead generation, proposals, fulfillment, post-sale support go to others).

Major account management

Major (key, national, global, house) accounts are important customers with multiple divisions in many locations, using uniform pricing and coordinated service. The average company manages about 75 key accounts; in a major account division the average manager handles nine. Large accounts get a cross-functional strategic account management team (P&G has about 300 staffers working on Walmart). It is growing because of buyer mergers, centralized purchasing, and product complexity. Major account managers act as the single point of contact, and accounts value added value (single billing, EDI links, priority shipping, customization, dedicated contact) more than price alone.

Exam angleCompute or reason about the agency-versus-company break-even (agency cheaper below SR, company force cheaper above). List the six sales tasks and identify the four types of sales force in a combined structure.

Size: the workload approach

Reps are among the most productive and most expensive assets; more reps raise both sales and costs. The streamlined workload method has five steps:

  1. Group customers into size classes by annual sales volume.
  2. Set desirable contact frequencies (calls per account per year) for each class.
  3. Multiply accounts in each class by its call frequency for total workload (calls per year).
  4. Determine the average number of calls one rep can make per year.
  5. Divide total calls by calls per rep to get the number of reps needed.

Worked example (from the chapter)

ClassAccountsFrequencyResult
A accounts1,000 accounts36 calls/year36,000 calls
B accounts2,000 accounts12 calls/year24,000 calls
Total workload60,000 calls/year
Rep capacity1,000 calls/year per full-time rep
Reps needed60,000 / 1,000 = 60 reps

Compensation: four components

ComponentPurpose
Fixed amount (salary)Satisfies the need for income stability.
Variable amountCommissions, quotas, bonus, profit sharing: stimulates and rewards effort.
Expense allowancesCover travel and entertaining on the company's behalf.
BenefitsPaid vacation, sickness/accident benefits, pensions, health and life insurance: security and job satisfaction.

Reps want income regularity, extra reward for above-average performance, and fair pay for experience; management wants control, economy, and simplicity. Fixed pay fits jobs with many non-selling duties or technically complex, team-based selling. Variable pay fits cyclical sales or sales that depend on individual initiative.

PlanAdvantagesDrawback
Straight salarySecure income; encourages non-selling tasks; reduces incentive to overstock customers; simple and lower turnover.Less motivation to push sales.
Straight commissionAttracts higher performers; more motivation; less supervision; controls selling costs.Emphasizes the sale over the relationship.
CombinationBenefits of both while limiting their disadvantages.More complex to design.

The third column's "More complex to design" is a study-aid inference; the chapter only says combination plans limit the disadvantages of the other two.

A current trend ties variable pay to gross profitability, customer satisfaction, and retention rather than just volume, and some firms reward team or company-wide performance. Microchip dropped commissions and sales actually increased.

Exam angleReproduce the workload calculation with new numbers (accounts x frequency, divided by capacity). Match each compensation plan to its advantages and downside.

Management covers four activities: recruiting, training, motivating, and evaluating (Figure 14.4).

Recruiting

Turnover costs lost sales, replacement and training expense, and pressure on remaining reps. Background, personality, and similar variables predict performance weakly. Better predictors are composite tests and assessment centers that simulate the working environment. IBM, Prudential, and P&G weight test scores heavily; Gillette claims tests reduced turnover. Reps also need marketing skills (analyzing sales data, measuring market potential, gathering intelligence, building plans).

Training and supervising

Customers expect deep product knowledge and ideas to improve operations, so training can run from a few weeks to several months. Commission-paid reps get less supervision than salaried reps covering specific accounts. In multilevel selling (Avon, Sara Lee, Virgin), independent distributors earn commission on their own sales and on sales of people they recruit and train. Left alone, reps over-serve known current customers, so firms specify prospecting time. Time-and-duty analysis shows reps where their day goes (planning, travel, waiting, selling, administration).

Technology (CRM, AI decision support, social networking) helps with front-end prospecting and back-end relationship management.

Motivating

Higher motivation leads to more effort, performance, rewards, and satisfaction, which loops back to raise motivation. A study found pay was the most valued reward, followed by promotion, personal growth, and sense of accomplishment; least valued were liking and respect, security, and recognition. Pay plans can vary by performer type:

TypeWhat motivates
StarsNo caps on commission, overachievement commissions beyond quota, prizes with multiple winners.
Core performersMultitier targets as stepping stones; contests with prizes of varying value.
LaggardsConsistent quarterly bonuses and social pressure.

Quotas come from the annual marketing plan and the sales forecast; regional quotas usually add up to more than the forecast to push performance. Problems: underestimating overpays reps; overestimating frustrates them or makes them quit; and quotas can push reps to ignore service, trading long-term satisfaction for short-term results, so some firms drop them.

Evaluating

Supervision combines feed-forward (telling reps what to do and motivating them) with feedback (information to judge performance). Sources: sales reports, personal observation, self-reports, customer letters and complaints, surveys, and conversations with other reps. Reports include the work plan (calls and routing planned in advance), the annual territory-marketing plan, and call reports, plus expense, near-business, lost-business, and local-conditions reports. Key indicators include calls per day, call time per contact, revenue per call, cost per call, entertainment cost per call, orders per hundred calls, new customers and lost customers per period, and sales force cost as a percentage of sales. Performance may be attributed to internal factors (effort, ability, strategy) or external factors (task and luck).

Exam angleMatch a performer type (star, core, laggard) to the right incentive; name the problems with quotas; and recognize feed-forward vs. feedback and the key sales performance indicators.

What it is and why it works

Direct marketing is the use of consumer-direct channels to reach and deliver goods and services to customers without intermediaries. Advantages: often more cost-effective than traditional marketing; direct contact yields information on needs, usage, and improvement areas; and it enables superior service and a stronger brand image. Direct marketers often seek a measurable response, typically an order (direct-order marketing). Growth is partly a response to the high cost of reaching business markets through a sales force.

Channels listed: direct mail, catalog marketing, telemarketing, kiosks, websites, and mobile devices. The chapter details the four below.

Direct mail

Sending an offer, announcement, or reminder to an individual consumer ("salespeople with wings"). Strengths: target selectivity, personalization, flexibility, early testing, and response measurement. Cost per thousand is higher than mass media but prospects are better. Weakness: crowded mailboxes.

RFM scoring selects customers by Recency (time since last purchase), Frequency (number of purchases), and Monetary amount spent. Chapter example for a leather jacket offer: customers whose last purchase was 30 to 60 days ago, who buy three to six times a year, and who have spent at least $100 since becoming customers; points are assigned by RFM level.

Best lists are past customers. Bought broker lists often have duplicates, incomplete data, and obsolete addresses; buy and test a sample first. Response rates understate impact: if 2% order a Samsonite luggage mailing, many more became aware, formed intentions, or spread word of mouth, so some firms also measure awareness, intention to buy, and word of mouth. Privacy concerns are growing (FTD lets customers choose which reminder emails to receive).

Catalog marketing, telemarketing, infomercials

ChannelKey facts
Catalog marketingFull-line, specialty consumer, and business catalogs, in print and online. W.W. Grainger prints a catalog of nearly 3,000 pages and also posts a searchable online version. Success depends on managing lists (avoid duplication and bad debts), controlling inventory, low returns through quality merchandise, and a distinctive image. The internet and catalog retailing industry: about 40,000 companies and $520 billion revenue.
TelemarketingTelephone and call centers to attract prospects, sell, and serve. Inbound = receiving calls; outbound = initiating calls. Has lost much effectiveness (still heavy in politics); B2B is increasing partly via videoconferencing. Robo-calls (autodialed prerecorded messages) are legal in the US only for relevant information (appointment reminders, flight changes, fraud alerts) or election campaigns, not for selling.
Infomercials30- and 60-minute TV spots combining selling power with information and entertainment; suit complicated, technologically advanced products needing explanation. Hits: Proactiv, P90X, and the George Foreman grill (each hundreds of millions in revenue). At-home shopping channels also sell goods this way.
Exam angleRFM is a favorite: define each letter and apply it to a customer profile. Also know inbound vs. outbound telemarketing, why robo-calls to sell are illegal, and why response rate understates direct mail impact.

Benefits to buyers and sellers

Buyers who are short of time and tired of traffic and parking value toll-free numbers, always-open websites, next-day delivery, and strong service; direct marketers also promote specialty items chains have dropped. Sellers can buy lists for almost any group, customize and personalize messages, build continuous relationships (new parents get mailings as the child grows), reach prospects when they want a solicitation, test media and messages, keep strategy less visible to competitors, and measure which campaigns are most profitable.

Integration and lifetime marketing

Direct marketing must be integrated with other communications and channels. Eddie Bauer, Lands' End, and the Franklin Mint built brands via mail and phone orders before opening stores and now cross-promote stores, catalogs, and websites. Successful direct marketers treat every interaction as a chance to up-sell, cross-sell, or deepen the relationship, and plan lifetime marketing based on customers' life events.
